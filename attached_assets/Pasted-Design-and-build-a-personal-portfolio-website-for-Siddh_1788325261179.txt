Design and build a personal portfolio website for **Siddhant Shambharkar**, a software engineer with a strong backend background, research experience, side projects, writing interests, and broader interests in travel, philosophy, psychology, storytelling, music, and personal growth.

The website should NOT look like a typical developer portfolio.

Avoid:

* dark themes
* black backgrounds
* terminal interfaces
* code snippets as decoration
* cyberpunk aesthetics
* neon gradients
* Jarvis/AI-dashboard style interfaces
* excessive glassmorphism
* generic startup landing-page design
* overly technical visual metaphors

Instead, create a website that feels like a mix of a **warm Sunday journal and a modern independent magazine**.

The overall emotional feeling should be:

warm
sunny
curious
colorful
calm
personal
slightly playful
thoughtful
human

It should feel like visiting someone's well-designed personal space rather than reading their resume.

## Core design direction

Use a warm off-white or cream background instead of pure white.

Suggested palette:

* warm cream / paper: #F7F1E5
* muted forest/sage green: #617A55
* warm mustard: #E6B655
* muted coral / terracotta: #D97757
* soft sky blue: #A8C9D8
* dark brown/charcoal for text: #2E2925

Do not use all colors everywhere. Use cream as the base and the other colors as editorial accents.

Use strong typography.

Combine:

* an elegant serif font for large headings
* a clean humanist sans-serif font for body text
* optionally one subtle handwritten font for very small annotations only

Typography should be one of the main visual elements.

The website should use:

* generous whitespace
* large editorial headings
* tasteful photography
* colored blocks
* subtle paper-like textures
* small botanical or hand-drawn accents
* slightly imperfect layouts where appropriate
* gentle scroll animations

Do not require complex custom illustrations.

Visual assets should be achievable using:

* CSS
* SVG doodles
* simple botanical illustrations
* photographs
* AI-generated textures or supporting artwork

## Most important UX requirement

The website must be extremely easy to understand and navigate.

A recruiter visiting for the first time should immediately understand:

1. Who Siddhant is
2. What he does professionally
3. His experience
4. His strongest projects
5. His background and education
6. Where to download his resume
7. How to contact him

Do not sacrifice usability for artistic design.

Use a simple top navigation:

Home
About
Work
Projects
Writing
Beyond Work
Contact

Keep the navigation visible or easily accessible.

Also include a prominent **Resume** button.

The website may feel like a magazine visually, but it should behave like a very simple conventional website.

## Homepage

The homepage hero should be warm, minimal and personal.

Do NOT start with:

"Hi, I'm Siddhant, a passionate software engineer..."

Avoid generic portfolio language.

Use something closer to:

**Siddhant Shambharkar**

I build things for a living, explore things out of curiosity, and occasionally write down what I notice.

Software Engineer · Builder · Curious Human

Include a warm natural portrait/photo on the page, preferably with daylight rather than a formal corporate headshot.

The hero should immediately contain two clear actions:

**See my work**

**About me**

Also have a smaller Resume link.

The hero should feel like the opening page of an independent magazine.

## Small introductory section

Immediately after the hero, add a short introduction.

Heading:

**A little about me**

Explain in 3–5 sentences that Siddhant is a software engineer whose professional work mainly revolves around backend engineering and building real-world systems, but that his interests go beyond software into research, writing, travel, psychology, philosophy and building personal projects.

Keep this section conversational rather than resume-like.

Nearby, add a small editorial card:

**Currently**

Building → something interesting
Learning → systems / engineering / another current topic
Reading → placeholder
Thinking about → placeholder
Based in → India

The values should be easy to update later.

## Work section

Heading:

**Things I do professionally**

This section must be straightforward because recruiters will care about it.

Show professional experience as clean editorial cards or a vertical timeline.

Each experience should clearly display:

Company
Role
Dates
Short description
2–4 meaningful contributions

Avoid giant lists of technologies.

Prioritize:

* problems solved
* ownership
* scale
* impact
* engineering decisions
* collaboration/business exposure

Add a clear link:

**View full experience →**

This can lead to a dedicated Work page.

## Projects section

Heading:

**Things I've made**

Projects should not look like GitHub repository cards.

Each project should feel like a mini magazine feature.

Each card should contain:

Project name
A very short human explanation
Why it exists
What Siddhant worked on
A few relevant technologies
Link if available

Use different but harmonious pastel backgrounds for project cards.

Include important personal/technical projects such as:

* RankUp
* mindbrew
* research / Neural Architecture Search work
* other significant engineering projects

Do not overload the homepage.

Show 3–4 featured projects and provide:

**See all projects →**

## Education and research

Create a section titled:

**The slightly academic chapter**

or

**When curiosity became research**

Include IIT Kharagpur and Siddhant's research work.

Explain the research in language understandable to a technically literate person without making the website feel like an academic CV.

Mention Neural Architecture Search, deep learning and optimization where appropriate.

Allow a visitor to explore deeper details if interested.

## Writing section

Heading:

**Things I've been thinking about**

Display writing/articles like magazine articles rather than blog cards.

For example:

CATEGORY

One-line description
3 min read

Topics can include:

engineering
technology

Writing should feel like an important part of the person's identity, not a secondary SEO blog.

## Beyond Work section

This section is important.

Heading:

**There's life outside the laptop**

or

**When I'm not building software**

Use a colorful, relaxed layout.

Show interests such as:

Travel
Guitar / music
Writing
Philosophy
Psychology
Storytelling
Photography / experiences
Learning random things

Do not display these as skill bars or icon grids.

Instead, use photographs, short sentences and small editorial cards.

Example:

**Travel**

I like places that force me to stop looking at a screen.

[photo]

**Philosophy & psychology**

Mostly an excuse to ask questions with increasingly inconvenient answers.

**Music**

Occasionally convincing six strings to cooperate.

Keep the tone intelligent, slightly humorous and natural.

## Personal photo strip

Include somewhere on the site a horizontal collection of 3–5 photographs.

They can represent:

travel
coffee
nature
friends/experiences
music
cities
quiet moments

Make it feel like someone inserted a few pages from their personal photo journal into the website.

Avoid an Instagram-grid appearance.

## Small "notes" elements

Throughout the site, occasionally include tiny handwritten/editorial annotations such as:

currently curious about this →

one of my favourite projects

this took longer than expected

would absolutely visit again

Use them sparingly.

They should add personality, not clutter.

## Contact section

Do not use corporate wording like:

"Let's collaborate and create innovative solutions."

Use something warmer.

For example:

**Say hello.**

Whether it's about work, something I've built, something I've written, or just an interesting idea — feel free to reach out.

Show:

Email
LinkedIn
GitHub
Resume

Keep it simple.

## Footer

Create a warm, minimal footer.

Example:

Made by Siddhant.

Still figuring things out.

© current year

Optionally add one small rotating/random line such as:

Currently somewhere between curiosity and overthinking.

Do not make the footer visually heavy.

## Animation

Animations should be subtle.

Use:

* gentle fade-up on scroll
* slight photo/card rotations
* slow underline animations
* subtle hover movement
* magazine-like reveal effects

Do NOT use:

* heavy parallax
* 3D scenes
* loading animations longer than necessary
* cursor gimmicks
* excessive page transitions
* animations that interfere with navigation

The website must remain fast.

## Responsive design

Mobile design is important.

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

On mobile:

* stack editorial layouts naturally
* keep typography readable
* reduce decorative elements
* preserve clear hierarchy
* keep navigation obvious
* make cards easy to scan

## Accessibility and performance

Use semantic HTML.

Maintain strong text/background contrast.

Provide keyboard navigation.

Provide alt text for images.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

Optimize images.

Avoid unnecessary JS libraries.

Aim for excellent Lighthouse performance.

## Final design philosophy

The final result should make a visitor think:

"This feels like an interesting person's personal website."

Not:

"This is another software developer portfolio."

Professional information should be extremely easy to find, but engineering should be only one part of Siddhant's identity.

The website should balance:

50% professional credibility
30% personality and interests
20% experimentation and charm

The design should remain visually cohesive throughout.

Think:

Sunday morning sunlight
a good magazine
a travel journal
coffee and books
small colorful details
warm photography
good typography
curiosity

Do not imitate those literally.

Use them only as the emotional and visual inspiration.

The result should feel mature, warm, colorful, optimistic and memorable while remaining simple enough that anyone — recruiter, engineer, friend or stranger — can navigate it immediately.
